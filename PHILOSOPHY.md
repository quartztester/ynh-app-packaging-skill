The Kiwix foundation is most known for its offline and downloadable versions of
Wikipedia. Shockingly, however, that only 15% of their downloads. Where then is
the remainder? In all of the other data sources they provide and 60% of their
traffic is not even in the top 500
(https://hub.kiwix.org/weblog/2024/03/our-very-long-tail/).

Chris Anderson's the long tail introduced the concept of selling less of more in
business. Kiwix provides less for more and their statistics align perfectly with
that theory.

In accordance with this evidence I postulate that the yunohost project and
community is on this same curve. While most of the popular applications get wide
support, the majority of individual voices and users have not had their means
met and the ability to host what they'd like flounders due to operational
capacity of the project and volunteer packagers. It is under this
interpretation that I believe the cost of not packaging an app is greater than
packaging one. As demand itself - in terms of forum messages or git issues will
show what is actually being used and encountering problems.

The yunohost project consistently gets wishlist requests for apps people wish
were packaged. That of who went out of their way to ask for the ability to host
something they might otherwise not have the technical chops for. There now sits
over 500+ wishlist apps that have not been packaged. This is clear indicative
demand for the project that has not been met and can easily be met by LLMs.

Human Hours can only do so much. A philosophy of package it anyway.*

1. I truly believe we are missing a significant portion of users that would like
   to host their infrastructure or apps but neglect it due to the backlog of our
   catalog. A user who would host it with docker is not the same as the people
   we are targeting and building for.
2. The environmental impact of the cumulative total hours a human would to
   support the project is substantially larger use of electricity than a GPU for
   an initial package of an app.
3. What's the worst case? The cost of packaging apps to be higher than that of
   maintaining them as an abandoned app is easier for a new maintainer to fix
   than start a package from scratch. I see over-commenting as a bigger issue
   than reliability as models get better over time.
